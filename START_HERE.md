# START HERE

Read this before anything else. It exists because the repo grew across many
investigations and most of them were negative results — which is the point, but
only if you know which is which.

## 1. The one thing to understand

**The engine does not have a proven edge.** Best pooled result across ~1,200
decision bars is **+1.47% mean 20-session return on the top-ranked name, with a
49.3% beat rate** — a coin flip. A broad index fund is the better bet on this
evidence.

That is not a failure of effort. It is the finding. Nine of the ten ideas
tested here were rejected by their own evidence, and the documentation of those
rejections is the most valuable thing in the repo. Do not "fix" the engine by
adding features; that is how it got to 39 modules.

regenerated by re-running the script. Nothing else depends on them surviving.

## 4. The five results worth knowing

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **The gates work; the score does not.** | Gates avoid the worst days. Among names that pass, the top score bucket is *not* better than the bottom (t = 0.59). |
| **Stops hurt significantly.** | Both a flat 7% stop and the engine's own structural stop lose ~3pp per 20-session hold, t ≈ −6. Win rate falls 52% → 27%. |
| **Named price-action patterns are noise.** | Volatility contraction t = 1.85, sweep/spring t = −0.51, all three frameworks measured on 499 names over 8 years. |
| **Durable levels reversed out of sample.** | The motivating anecdote said "skip stocks at old resistance." Pooled, touching an old level is mildly *positive* (t = +2.08). |
| **The biggest loss was a newswire event.** | BAJAJ-AUTO fell 7.6% on 2026-10-01 when September domestic two-wheeler sales printed −12% YoY. OHLCV cannot contain that. |

## 5. Rules the code enforces — do not break these

1. **No source file over 300 lines.** `find protocol scripts tests -name "*.py" -exec wc -l {} +`
2. **No participant-identity inference.** No claims about accumulation, distribution,
   supply, demand, or absorption — OHLCV cannot tell you who traded. There is a test
   for this. Framework names (Wyckoff, ICT, Minervini) appear **only in prose as
   citations**; every column is price-only (`durable_high`, `level_gap`).
3. **All windows lagged ≥ 1 session.** Enforced by `protocol/audit.py` with a
   `DECLARED_EXEMPTIONS` mechanism. Stale exemptions are flagged.
4. **A short panel yields NaN, never a guess.** `levels.sufficiency(500)` reports the
   shortfall instead of producing a plausible wrong number.
5. **Thresholds only in `config/protocol_v2.yaml`.**
6. **Labels are `HISTORICAL_CANDIDATE` / `RESEARCH_CANDIDATE`, never "BUY".**

## 6. Before you add a feature

You will be tempted. Resist. With ~248 usable sessions in the original panel and
a gates layer already cutting 937,127 observations to 5,907, every additional
filter multiplies the multiple-testing problem. `protocol/inference.py` runs
Bonferroni and BH correction precisely because this has already bitten.

**Measure first, build second.** `scripts/pa_signals.py` is the template: it
audits candidate ideas without touching the engine, so a weak idea costs one run
and no code.

## 7. A warning about numbers in this repo

Ten errors are logged in `ENGINE_REVIEW.md` §9. Five share one cause: **reaching
for a number before establishing what it means.** They produced a +89% mean
return, a +72.55% mean, and a 99.9% hit rate. All were caught by a sanity check,
not by the test suite, which passed throughout.

**If a figure here looks impossible, it is wrong before you ask why.** Check the
arithmetic, then the reasoning.
