⚡ Relative Strength (RS) vs 20-Day Return in Layman's Terms

×

1. Why 20-Day Return is Misleading (The 100m Sprint Trap)

A stock that surged +20% in the last 20 days is often like a sprinter who just finished running at top speed — exhausted and gasping for air. In quantitative finance, short bursts tend to mean-revert (reverse). In our 8-year NSE backtest, raw 20-day return actually had a negative Information Coefficient (IC = -0.0128). Chasing 20-day surges buys tops.

2. What is Relative Strength (RS Rating)? (Grading on a Curve)

Instead of looking at isolated short-term gains, Relative Strength compares a stock against every other stock in the entire market (Nifty 500 universe) over a full year (40% 1-year, 20% 6-month, 20% 3-month, 20% 1-month). An RS Rating of 98 means the stock has outperformed 98% of all NSE stocks. Even if the broader market crashes, true RS leaders refuse to go down, and when the market turns, they explode upwards.

3. What Benchmark Do We Use? (Nifty 500 vs Nifty 50)

We use the Equal-Weight NSE Universe Index (Nifty 500 Equivalent) as the primary benchmark. Why? Nifty 50 is only 50 giant corporations (Reliance, HDFC Bank, TCS). Comparing midcaps and smallcaps to Nifty 50 is comparing apples to watermelons. The broad Nifty 500 benchmark represents 96% of the Indian stock market, giving an honest, unbiased comparative standard.

🌊 Wyckoff Effort vs Result & Price Acceptance Guide

×

1. Richard Wyckoff's Law of Effort vs. Result

In quantitative Volume Spread Analysis (VSA), Effort is measured by Relative Volume (RVOL) and traded turnover, while Result is measured by the Candlestick Spread and Closing Range (CR):

  • Wyckoff Absorption (High Effort → High Result): High volume (RVOL ≥ 2.0x) paired with a close in the upper 30% of the session (CR ≥ 70%). Institutional buyers aggressively absorb all overhead supply at the resistance ceiling and push through.
  • Effort vs Result Churn (Exhaustion Trap): Heavy volume (RVOL ≥ 3.0x) but weak close (CR ≤ 50%). High effort failed to yield upward progress, warning that institutional sellers are quietly dumping inventory into retail breakout orders.
  • Supply Vacuum Glide (Low Effort → Clean Result): Price glides cleanly into new highs on low volume (RVOL ≤ 1.0x). Indicates that sellers have completely vanished from the market.

2. The 4-State Price Acceptance (PA) Machine (protocol/pa.py)

Tracks how price behaves immediately upon penetrating key 20-day or 52-week resistance:

  • State A (Accepted Expansion): Penetrates resistance, closes above it, holding ≥60% of the breakout distance (Retention ≥ 60%) and closing near the highs (CR ≥ 60%). The highest mathematical edge setup.
  • State B (Pending Acceptance): Closed above resistance, but closed mid-bar. Requires next-day confirmation.
  • State D (Failed Acceptance / Upthrust): Pierced resistance intraday, but surrendered it by the close. A classic bull trap.

3. Support & Resistance Memory (protocol/levels.py)

Most screeners only track 52-week highs. Our engine tracks Durable Highs spanning 1 to 3 years (260 to 760 sessions), alerting when a stock breaks out over multi-year ceilings that standard rolling windows forget.
